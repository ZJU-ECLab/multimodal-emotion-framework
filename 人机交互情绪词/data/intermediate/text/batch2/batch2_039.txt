    The term of Affective Computing was coined in the early of 90s to describe the field of “Computing that relates
to, arises from, or deliberately influences emotions” 6 . Affective Computing framework allows a system or a computer
to capture, process, interpret and convey emotions. This research aims to implement Affective Computing as part of
game design by capturing, processing, and interpreting the player’s emotions to enhance the player’s experience in
the game. However, it is a cumbersome task to blend the emotions factor to games without decreasing the experiences
that have been offered by the game itself. Interpreting wrong emotions, or conveying inappropriate emotions resulted
in the breaking of the interaction, leading to the diminishing of the player game experience. The research questions
of using Affective Computing as the part of game design are: RQ1: Which emotions should be captured to enhance
the players game experience?; RQ2: How to fuse the elements of the emotions in the game?; and RQ3: Will affective
computing enhance the players game experience? In this paper, we argue by implementing affective computing in the
game will statistically enhance the players game experiences (H1 ). The paper is organised as follows: Next section
describes the recent works done in the field of affective computing in the game as well as what have been done to
increase the game player experiences. The proposed method of this research is thoroughly illustrated in the Proposed
Method section. Moreover, the discussion of the results is comprehensively described in the Results and Discussion
section. Finally, the conclusion of the implication of the research and the future research direction are described in the
last section.


2. Recent Work

    While Affective Computing topic has been around for more than two decades, implementing Affective Computing
is still considered as in the infancy state. The ultimate goal of the game is to make players satisfy when, and after
playing the game. The game designers are competing to create a game that offers the best game player experiences.
This section aims to comprehensively describe the recent work done in enhancing game player experiences. Subsection
Enhancing Player Experience explains the efforts done to enhance the player experiences in the game. In contrast,
subsection Affective Computing in Game describes what the recent implementation in affective computing is, what is
affective computing in the game means, and how it helps the game to improve the game player experiences.

2.1. Enhancing Player Experience

   The paramount aspect when playing a game is the player’s experiences. Positive game experiences lead to the
increase of the game popularity and vice versa. Several techniques have been done to enhance the player experiences
in the game. The renowned methods to increase the player experiences in the game are, but not limited to: building
a realistic environment, creating a masterpiece story, and crafting complex scenarios, objects, and characters in the
game. Those methods are currently being implemented by most of the commercial game companies. In addition
to those techniques, recently researchers and game designers are also working in collaborating social aspect in the
game 7,3,4 , implementing mixed reality concept in the game 8 , and integrating emotions (i.e. affect) as the element of
game design 9,3,4,5,2 .
   Social presence in digital game worlds evidently enhances the players experience in the game. Several research
have shown, by imbuing social aspects in the game, statistically increasing the game player experiences both as
individual and as a group 7,3,4 . Similar results also can be concluded from the research done by Hudson, et al. 10 ,
where social interaction in the game increasing in satisfaction and loyalty, leading to the increasing of game player
experiences. Inculcating social aspects also empirically enhance the player’s immersiveness and engagement, resulting
in a positive game player experiences. Another method to increasing game player experience is to integrating mixed
reality concept in the game, where the players are able to interact with objects in both realities (i.e. the real world)
and virtual location (i.e. the game worlds). Research has shown that using location-based system (e.g. GPS) in the

game, where the locations in the game world blend into locations in the real world, were increasing the players
experiences 11,12,8,13 . With GPS, the devices (e.g. mobile phones) are able to point the location of the players exactly,
and the game will adjust the game states, worlds, and events based on the player’s location. Eventually, it will increase
the player’s immersiveness and engagement during the game. Imbuing emotions to the game design elements also
evidently enhancing the game player experiences. Similar to the other methods, emotions play an important part in
the player’s immersiveness and engagement during the game. Affect system can be implemented to regulate NPC’s
emotions system 3,4 , building dynamic game balancing 5,2 , and conveying the emotions 9 . A comprehensive discussion
on affective computing in the game is thoroughly presented in the next subsection.

2.2. Affective Computing in Game

   In contrast with the affective computing framework implementation in general (e.g. in the health system, or security
system), affective computing framework implementation in a game environment is still in the state of infancy. It is
a cumbersome task to implement affect (i.e. emotions) to a socially ignorant computer 6,4 . Moreover, research in
implementing Affective Computing in the game is still considered as blue-sky research, where the implementation in
a real-world is still a challenge. There are several variables and aspects of the affect that are still unknown for its effect
on the game player experiences. The combination of variables and aspects in the affect also still have not all been
explored yet. Hence, this makes the implementation of affective computing in the game is still either simple to avoid
unknown complex effect in the game player experiences, or done in an ad-hoc manner, where the variables settings
are only best performed in particular scenarios in the game. Affective computing framework can be implemented in
several methods in the game: capture and recognising the emotions, process the emotions and conveying the emotions.
Emotions can be captured via facial expressions, voice prosody (e.g. pitch, intonation, and pattern) or the combination
of those two 3,4 . Emotions can be processed using computational models of emotions 14,15,3,4 so the NPCs are able
to regulate their emotions bases on their environments and act based on their regulated emotions 3,4 . In addition,
the emotions also can be conveyed by the NPCs via their facial expressions, actions, and also voice prosody 3,4,16 .
Most of the results of affective computing framework implementation in the game were consistent with the initial
alternative hypothesis where a game with affective computing involved, statistically providing more immersiveness
and engagements aspects, leading to the increasing the game player experiences.

   There are four phases in the proposed methods of this research (see Fig.1): Literature review and user requirements
gathering, game design, game development, and evaluation. As the first step, a literature review was performed to
understand what has been done in this area, and what are the problems and gaps. In this research. This research aims
to implement Affective Computing as part of game design by capturing, processing, and interpreting the player’s
emotions to enhance the player’s experience in the game. From the literature, it can be concluded that it is quite a
challenging task to blend the emotions factor to the games without decreasing the experiences that have been offered
by the games itself. Hence, a simple yet rich interaction game was proposed in this research. A pet simulation game
was proposed as a platform to evaluate the implementation of Affective Computing as part of game design. An initial
user requirements gathering was performed using a questionnaire to understand the needs of players. Next step is to
design the game, where the mechanics, interactions, also Affective Computing aspect were designed in this step. The

4. Results and Discussion

    A pet simulation game was designed and developed to evaluate the implementation of Affective Computing in
game. The Affective Computing system imbued in the game is responsible for capturing, processing, and interpret-
ing the player’s emotions via their facial expressions when they are playing the game. The rationale to choose a pet
simulation game is that the game itself quite simple yet offer rich interaction between players and the game environ-
ments (e.g. NPCs, objects, worlds). The game was developed in the PC (Personal Computer) platform, as a proof of
concept before deploying it to a mobile phone, where most of the players will enjoy playing this kind of game. An
initial questionnaire to gather the user requirements was conducted to 100 respondents via the online form with the
majority of male respondents (59%), aged 18-25 (72%), students (77%), fluent in computer (100%), and spent more
than 5 hours a day in a computer (54%), like to play game (82%). The result indicates that most of the people prefer
to play a virtual pet game in a mobile device (72%), but they also do not mind if playing in a computer (62%). Most
of the respondents prefer cat as their virtual pet (59%), and interested in playing a game with the Affective Computing
aspect implemented in the game (92%). Based on the results, the game proposes in this research as the evaluation and
exploration tool is A pet simulation game, with a cat as the virtual pet, and deployed in a PC. The goal of the game
is to take care of their virtual cat by providing foods, play with it, take care if the pet is sick, maintain the virtual cat
hygiene. Two versions of the game were deployed with a slightly different in the core mechanics; one was with Af-
fective Computing system imbued in the game, the other one was with-out Affective Computing system implemented

    A set of experiments with questionnaire was designed to evaluate, answer RQ3:, and test the hypotheses statis-
tically (H0 : Affective Computing implementation in the game is not increasing the players game experiences, and
H1 : Affective Computing implementation in the game is increasing the players game experiences). A total of 50 re-
spondents participated in the evaluation phase, with a total of 100 sessions conducted. Each respondent played both
Game A and B with randomised order between games. Every time the respondent finished with either game, they
were asked to fill in a five Likert scale (0-4) questionnaire inspired by Game Experience Questionnaire 19 and Player
Immersiveness Questionnaire 20 . A Shapiro-Wilk Normality test was applied to the questionnaire results, where the
result indicates that the data is more likely to come from a non-normal population (W = 0.69, p < 0.01). Hence, a
non-rank statistic test was applied to test the hypotheses. Fig. 4 right side indicates the box plot of the average score in
the questionnaire for both groups (i.e. game A and game B). The result indicates that the group played with the game
with FER (i.e. game A) scored significantly higher in the game experiences score compared to the game with-out FER
(i,e. game B). A Wilcoxon signed-rank test was applied to the questionnaire results to test the hypotheses statistically.
In overall, the game with FER imbued in the system provide statistically higher game experiences score compared
to the game with-out FER (p < 0.01). Fig. 4 left side illustrates the questionnaire score from respondents in both
groups. The result concludes that most of the game experiences score in the game with FER system implemented
was statistically increased compared to the one with-out the FER system implemented, except for Q3 (p = 0.06), and
Q9 (p = 0.08). Whereas, Q1, Q2, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7, Q8, Q11, Q12, Q13 have significant statistically increase in the
game with FER system imbued compare to the one with-out the system (p < 0.01, and Q10 with p < 0.05). Thus this
answers the research question RQ3 posed in this research.


5. Conclusion and Future Work

   This research aims to imbued Affective Computing components to the game by using Facial Expression Recogni-
tion to capture, process, and interpret the player’s emotions as game input to enhance the player’s experience in the
game. In the preliminary research done, to answer research question RQ1: Which emotions should be captured to
enhance the players game experience?, three emotions were chosen to be captured from the player’s facial expres-
sions via camera. The three expressions chosen were relatively easy yet challenging to be expressed by the user. This
condition was perfect for designing proper game balancing. To answer question RQ2: How to fuse the elements of
the emotions in the game?, the facial expression recognition system then was implemented to the game as a game
input in the mini-games. To test the hypotheses posed in the research and to answer RQ3: Will affective computing
enhance the players game experience?, a PC pet simulation game was designed and developed. There are two types
of games to be played by two groups of identical respondents. The results indicated that the players who played the

compared to the one with-out FER imbued. The exceptions were to the aspect of the graphics and imagery of the game

no differences between two games, and the players were not feeling their competences were statistically increasing

two games; they felt that they were faster to reach the game’s target (Q10). A comprehensive evaluation and analysis

For the future research directions, other combinations of emotions that can be implemented to the game system and
explore which one is the best combination to increase the player game experiences. Moreover, the game is planned to
be deployed in a mobile device in the future.
